7.1 Systems and Environments

🎯 What is a System?

In physics, a system is a small portion of the universe that we choose to study. The environment is everything outside the system that can interact with it.

📌 A System Can Be:

  • A single object or particle - Example: A baseball flying through the air
  • A collection of objects - Example: The Earth-Moon system
  • A region of space - Example: Gas inside an engine cylinder
  • Variable in size and shape - Example: A rubber ball deforming when hitting a wall

💡 What is Energy?

Energy is the ability to do work. It is a scalar quantity measured in Joules (J).

Types of Energy:

Energy Type Description Example
Kinetic Energy \(K\) Energy of motion Moving car, flowing river
Potential Energy \(U\) Stored energy due to position Book on a shelf, compressed spring
Thermal Energy Internal energy due to temperature Hot coffee, friction-heated brakes
Chemical Energy Stored in molecular bonds Battery, food, gasoline

📖 Example 1: Identifying Systems

Scenario: A person throws a ball upward.

Different System Choices:

  1. System = Ball only
    Environment includes: Earth (gravity), air (resistance), person's hand initially
  2. System = Ball + Earth
    Environment includes: air, person's hand
  3. System = Ball + Earth + Air
    Environment includes: person's hand, surroundings
💡 Key Insight: The choice of system affects how we analyze energy transfers. Choose the system that makes the problem simplest!

7.2 Work Done by a Constant Force

📐 Definition of Work

Work is done when a force causes a displacement. For a constant force, work is defined as:

$$ W = F d \cos(\theta) $$

Where:

  • \(W\) = Work done (Joules, J)
  • \(F\) = Magnitude of the force (Newtons, N)
  • \(d\) = Magnitude of the displacement (meters, m)
  • \(\theta\) = Angle between force and displacement vectors

⚠️ SI Unit of Work:

1 Joule (J) = 1 Newton·meter (N·m) = 1 kg·m²/s²

🎨 Visual Understanding

Force at different angles relative to displacement

📊 Work in Different Scenarios

Angle \(\theta\) \(\cos(\theta)\) Work Meaning
\(0^\circ\) 1 \(W = F d\) (Maximum) Force parallel to motion
\(0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ\) Positive \(W > 0\) Force has component along motion
\(90^\circ\) 0 \(W = 0\) Force perpendicular to motion
\(90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ\) Negative \(W < 0\) Force opposes motion
\(180^\circ\) -1 \(W = -F d\) (Minimum) Force directly opposes motion

📖 Example 2: Pushing a Box

Problem: A person pushes a 20 kg box with a force of 100 N at an angle of 30° below the horizontal. The box moves 5 m horizontally. Calculate the work done by:

  1. The applied force
  2. Gravity
  3. Normal force
Step 1: Work by Applied Force
Given: \(F = 100 \text{ N}\), \(d = 5 \text{ m}\), \(\theta = 30^\circ\)
$$ W = F d \cos(\theta) $$ $$ W = 100 \times 5 \times \cos(30^\circ) $$ $$ W = 100 \times 5 \times 0.866 $$ \(W \approx 433 \text{ J}\)
Step 2: Work by Gravity
Gravity acts vertically downward, displacement is horizontal
\(\theta = 90^\circ\) between force and displacement
$$ W = mg d \cos(90^\circ) $$ $$ W = mg d \times 0 $$ \(W = 0 \text{ J}\)
Step 3: Work by Normal Force
Normal force acts vertically upward, displacement is horizontal
\(\theta = 90^\circ\)
\(W = 0 \text{ J}\)
💡 Key Insight: Only forces with components along the displacement do work!

📖 Example 3: Lifting an Object

Problem: You lift a 5 kg book from the floor to a shelf 2 m high at constant velocity. Calculate:

  1. Work done by you
  2. Work done by gravity
  3. Net work
Given:
\(m = 5 \text{ kg}\), \(d = 2 \text{ m}\), \(g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2\)
Constant velocity means \(a = 0\), so \(F_{\text{applied}} = mg\)
Step 1: Work by You
Your force upward = \(mg = 5 \times 10 = 50 \text{ N}\)
Displacement upward = \(2 \text{ m}\)
\(\theta = 0^\circ\) (same direction)
$$ W = 50 \times 2 \times \cos(0^\circ) $$ \(W = 100 \text{ J}\)
Step 2: Work by Gravity
Force = \(mg = 50 \text{ N}\) (downward)
Displacement = \(2 \text{ m}\) (upward)
\(\theta = 180^\circ\) (opposite directions)
$$ W = 50 \times 2 \times \cos(180^\circ) $$ $$ W = 50 \times 2 \times (-1) $$ \(W = -100 \text{ J}\)
Step 3: Net Work
$$ W_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{you}} + W_{\text{gravity}} $$ $$ W_{\text{net}} = 100 + (-100) $$ \(W_{\text{net}} = 0 \text{ J}\)
⚠️ Important: Zero net work means no change in kinetic energy (constant velocity)!

7.3 The Scalar Product of Two Vectors

🔢 Dot Product (Scalar Product)

The scalar product (or dot product) of two vectors A and B is a scalar quantity defined as:

$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = AB \cos(\theta) $$

Or in component form:

$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z $$

📏 Properties of the Dot Product

  1. Commutative: \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{A}\)
  2. Distributive: \(\mathbf{A} \cdot (\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} + \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{C}\)
  3. With scalar: \((c\mathbf{A}) \cdot \mathbf{B} = c(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B})\)
  4. Self product: \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{A} = A^2\)

🎯 Unit Vector Dot Products

Product Result Reason
\(\mathbf{\hat{i}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{i}}\) 1 Parallel vectors (\(\theta = 0^\circ\))
\(\mathbf{\hat{j}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{j}}\) 1 Parallel vectors (\(\theta = 0^\circ\))
\(\mathbf{\hat{k}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{k}}\) 1 Parallel vectors (\(\theta = 0^\circ\))
\(\mathbf{\hat{i}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{j}}\) 0 Perpendicular vectors (\(\theta = 90^\circ\))
\(\mathbf{\hat{i}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{k}}\) 0 Perpendicular vectors (\(\theta = 90^\circ\))
\(\mathbf{\hat{j}} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{k}}\) 0 Perpendicular vectors (\(\theta = 90^\circ\))

⚡ Work as a Dot Product

Using the dot product, we can write work more elegantly:

$$ W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d} $$

This is equivalent to: W = F d cos(θ)

📖 Example 4: Computing Dot Products

Problem: Given A = 3i + 4j and B = 2i - j, find:

  1. A · B
  2. The angle between them
Step 1: Calculate Dot Product
$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = A_x B_x + A_y B_y $$ $$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = (3)(2) + (4)(-1) $$ $$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = 6 - 4 $$ \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = 2\)
Step 2: Find Magnitudes
\(|\mathbf{A}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\)
\(|\mathbf{B}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24\)
Step 3: Find Angle
$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = |\mathbf{A}| |\mathbf{B}| \cos(\theta) $$ $$ 2 = (5)(2.24) \cos(\theta) $$ $$ \cos(\theta) = \frac{2}{11.2} \approx 0.179 $$ \(\theta \approx 79.7^\circ\)

📖 Example 5: Work Using Vectors

Problem: A force F = 10i + 5j N displaces an object through d = 3i + 4j m. Find the work done.

Solution:
$$ W = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{d} $$ $$ W = F_x d_x + F_y d_y $$ $$ W = (10)(3) + (5)(4) $$ $$ W = 30 + 20 $$ \(W = 50 \text{ J}\)
💡 Tip: Using component form is often easier than finding the angle!

7.4 Work Done by a Varying Force

📈 Variable Force Work

When force varies with position, we must use integration:

$$ W = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F(x) dx $$

Physical Meaning: Work equals the area under the Force vs. Position graph!

🔗 Work Done by a Spring (Hooke's Law)

A spring exerts a force proportional to its displacement from equilibrium:

$$ F_{\text{spring}} = -kx $$

Where:

  • \(k\) = spring constant (N/m) - measure of stiffness
  • \(x\) = displacement from equilibrium (m)
  • The negative sign indicates the force opposes displacement

⚙️ Spring Constant \(k\):

  • Large \(k\) → Stiff spring (hard to compress/stretch)
  • Small \(k\) → Soft spring (easy to compress/stretch)

🧮 Deriving Work Done on a Spring

To find work done in stretching/compressing a spring from \(x_i\) to \(x_f\):

$$ W = \int F dx = \int (-kx) dx $$ $$ W = -k \int x dx $$ $$ W = -k \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right] $$ $$ W = \frac{1}{2} k x_i^2 - \frac{1}{2} k x_f^2 $$

From equilibrium (\(x_i = 0\)) to position \(x_f\):

$$ W = -\frac{1}{2} k x_f^2 $$

The negative sign indicates we do negative work if we go with the spring force, or positive work if we go against it.

📖 Example 6: Compressing a Spring

Problem: A spring with k = 500 N/m is compressed from its equilibrium position by 10 cm. How much work is done by:

  1. The external force (you) compressing it?
  2. The spring force?
Given:
\(k = 500 \text{ N/m}\)
\(x_i = 0 \text{ m}\) (equilibrium)
\(x_f = -0.10 \text{ m}\) (compressed, taking compression as negative)
Step 1: Work by Spring Force
$$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}k(x_i^2 - x_f^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}(500)(0^2 - (-0.10)^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}(500)(0 - 0.01) $$ $$ W_{\text{spring}} = 250(-0.01) $$ \(W_{\text{spring}} = -2.5 \text{ J}\)
Step 2: Work by External Force
By energy conservation, external work must equal negative of spring work:
\(W_{\text{external}} = -W_{\text{spring}}\)
\(W_{\text{external}} = +2.5 \text{ J}\)
💡 Interpretation:
• Spring does negative work (opposes compression)
• You do positive work (supply energy to compress)
• Energy stored in spring = 2.5 J

📖 Example 7: Spring Released from Compression

Problem: The spring from Example 6 is released. How much work does the spring force do as it returns to equilibrium?

Solution:
\(x_i = -0.10 \text{ m}\) (compressed)
\(x_f = 0 \text{ m}\) (equilibrium)

$$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}k(x_i^2 - x_f^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}(500)((-0.10)^2 - 0^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2}(500)(0.01) $$ \(W_{\text{spring}} = +2.5 \text{ J}\)
⚠️ Notice: Now the spring does positive work! It pushes the object back, transferring the stored energy.

7.5 Kinetic Energy and the Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem

🏃 Kinetic Energy (KE)

Kinetic energy is the energy associated with the motion of an object.

$$ K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 $$

Where:

  • \(K\) = Kinetic Energy (Joules, J)
  • \(m\) = Mass (kg)
  • \(v\) = speed (magnitude of velocity) (m/s)

Note: Kinetic Energy is a scalar quantity and is always non-negative (\(K \geq 0\)).

✨ Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem

When work is done on a system and the only change is in its speed:

$$ W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i $$
$$ W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2} m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} m v_i^2 $$
  • Positive Net Work (\(W_{\text{net}} > 0\)): Energy added → Speed increases
  • Negative Net Work (\(W_{\text{net}} < 0\)): Energy removed → Speed decreases
  • Zero Net Work (\(W_{\text{net}} = 0\)): No change in speed

📖 Example 8: Stopping a Car

Problem: A 1000 kg car traveling at 20 m/s skids to a stop. How much work does friction do on the car?

Given:
\(m = 1000 \text{ kg}\)
\(v_i = 20 \text{ m/s}\)
\(v_f = 0 \text{ m/s}\) (stopped)
Use Work-KE Theorem:
$$ W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i $$ $$ W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}m(v_f^2) - \frac{1}{2}m(v_i^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{net}} = 0 - \frac{1}{2}(1000)(20^2) $$ $$ W_{\text{net}} = -500(400) $$ \(W_{\text{net}} = -200,000 \text{ J} = -200 \text{ kJ}\)

7.6 Potential Energy of a System

🔋 Potential Energy (PE)

Potential energy is energy stored in a system due to the configuration (position) of its parts. It requires a system of at least two objects interacting via a force.

🌍 Gravitational Potential Energy (\(U_g\))

Energy stored in the Earth-Object system due to height.

$$ U_g = mgy $$

Where:

  • \(m\) = mass (kg)
  • \(g\) = acceleration due to gravity (\(9.8 \text{ m/s}^2\))
  • \(y\) = height above a reference point (m)
Reference Point: You can choose \(y=0\) anywhere! Only changes in potential energy (\(\Delta U\)) are physically significant.

🌀 Elastic Potential Energy (\(U_s\))

Energy stored in a deformed spring (or elastic material).

$$ U_s = \frac{1}{2} k x^2 $$

Where \(x\) is displacement from equilibrium.

Note: \(U_s\) is always positive (since \(x^2\) is always positive).

🔄 Internal Energy

Energy associated with the temperature and structure of a system (friction creates internal energy). When friction acts within a system:

$$ \Delta E_{\text{int}} = f_k d $$

Where \(f_k\) is kinetic friction and \(d\) is the path length.

Spring System: U = ½kx²
• Parabolic curve
• Single stable equilibrium at x = 0
• Force: F = -kx (proportional to displacement)

7.7 Conservative and Nonconservative Forces

🛡️ Conservative Forces

A force is conservative if the work it does on a particle moving between two points is independent of the path taken.

Properties:

  1. Work depends only on initial and final positions.
  2. Work done moving around any closed loop is zero (\(W_{\text{closed}} = 0\)).
  3. We can define a Potential Energy (\(U\)) for it.

Examples: Gravity, Spring force, Electric force.

⛔ Nonconservative Forces

Work depends on the path taken. Mechanical energy is often converted to internal energy (heat).

Examples: Friction, Air resistance, Drag.

Work Relations:

Conservative Force: $$ W_A = W_B = W_C $$

Nonconservative Force: $$ W_A < W_B < W_C $$

Visual Proof: For gravity (conservative), lifting a ball straight up (Path A) requires same work as pushing it up a ramp (Path B) to the same height (ignoring friction).

⚖️ Mechanical Energy

The total mechanical energy of a system is:

$$ E_{\text{mech}} = K + U $$

If only conservative forces act, mechanical energy is conserved:

$$ \Delta E_{\text{mech}} = 0 \Rightarrow K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f $$

If nonconservative forces (like friction) act:

$$ W_{\text{nc}} = \Delta E_{\text{mech}} = \Delta K + \Delta U $$

7.8 Relationship Between Conservative Forces and Potential Energy

🔗 Force from Potential Energy

Conservative forces always drive a system toward lower potential energy. The force is the negative derivative (slope) of the potential energy function.

$$ F_x = - \frac{dU}{dx} $$
  • Steep slope (large \(dU/dx\)) → Strong force
  • Flat slope (\(dU/dx = 0\)) → Zero force (Equilibrium)
  • Positive slope → Force is negative (pushes left)
  • Negative slope → Force is positive (pushes right)

⚖️ Types of Equilibrium

Type Condition Description
Stable \(\frac{dU}{dx} = 0\), \(U\) is min (valley) System returns to position if displaced (like a ball in a bowl).
Unstable \(\frac{dU}{dx} = 0\), \(U\) is max (peak) System speeds away if displaced (like a ball on a hill).
Neutral \(\frac{dU}{dx} = 0\), \(U\) is constant System stays put if displaced (ball on flat table).

📖 Example 9: Lennard-Jones Potential

The potential energy between two atoms in a molecule is often modeled as:

$$ U(r) = \frac{A}{r^{12}} - \frac{B}{r^6} $$

The force is \(F(r) = -dU/dr\). The atoms settle at the distance \(r\) where force is zero (minimum \(U\)) - this is the bond length!

🧩 Topic 1: Systems & Energy Types

Q1: A cyclist moves down a hill. If we define the system as "Cyclist + Earth", is the gravitational force an external or internal force?

Internal. Since the Earth is part of the system, the interaction between the Earth and the cyclist (gravity) is internal.

Q2: Identify the energy transformation: A battery-operated toy car accelerates up a ramp.

Chemical Potential Energy (Battery) → Electrical Energy → Kinetic Energy (Motion) + Gravitational Potential Energy (Height) + Thermal Energy (Friction).

Q3: True or False: A system can have potential energy even if it consists of only a single isolated particle.

False. Potential energy exists only between interacting objects (like Earth + Stone). A single particle can only have Kinetic Energy or Rest Energy.

Q4: Which of the following is NOT a unit of energy? (a) Joule (b) Newton-meter (c) Watt (d) \(\text{kg} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2\)

(c) Watt. Watt is a unit of Power (Joules per second). All others are units of Energy.

Q5: If the environment does positive work on a system, what happens to the system's total energy?

Since \(W_{\text{ext}} = \Delta E_{\text{sys}}\), positive work increases the total energy of the system.

Q5a: A rocket burns fuel in space. Define the system as "rocket + exhaust gases". What energy transformation occurs?

Chemical PE → Kinetic Energy. The chemical energy stored in the fuel is converted to kinetic energy of both the rocket and exhaust gases. Since the system includes both, momentum is conserved internally.

Q5b: A compressed spring is placed between two carts on a frictionless surface and released. If the system is "both carts + spring", is the spring's force internal or external?

Internal. The spring is part of the system, so any force it exerts is an internal force.

Q5c: Which represents energy transfer, not transformation?
(a) A ball falling
(b) A hot object warming a cold one
(c) A battery powering a motor
(d) A spring launching a ball

(b) A hot object warming a cold one. This is heat transfer between objects, not energy transformation.

Q5d: A system loses \(50 \text{ J}\) of energy to the environment. What is \(W_{\text{env}}\)?

\(W_{\text{env}} = \Delta E_{\text{sys}} = -50 \text{ J}\). Negative work means energy leaves the system.

Q5e: In a car crash, KE is mainly converted to:
(a) Gravitational PE (b) Elastic PE (c) Internal Energy + Sound (d) Chemical Energy

(c) Internal Energy + Sound. The kinetic energy goes into deforming metal (internal energy), heating, and sound.

📐 Topic 2: Work (Constant Force) & Dot Products

Q6: Calculate the work done by a force \(F = 20 \text{ N}\) pushing a box \(3 \text{ m}\) across a floor at an angle of \(60^\circ\) below the horizontal.

$$ W = F d \cos(\theta) = 20(3) \cos(60^\circ) = 60(0.5) = 30 \text{ J} $$

Q7: A waiter carries a tray of food across a room at constant velocity. Is the waiter doing work on the tray?

No. The force (Normal force) is vertical (up), while the displacement is horizontal. Since \(\theta = 90^\circ\), \(W = F d \cos(90^\circ) = 0\).

Q8: Calculate the dot product of \(\mathbf{A} = 3\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 2\mathbf{\hat{j}}\) and \(\mathbf{B} = -1\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 4\mathbf{\hat{j}}\).

$$ \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = (3)(-1) + (2)(4) = -3 + 8 = 5 $$

Q9: If \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0\) and neither vector is zero, what is the geometric relationship between them?

They are perpendicular (orthogonal), because \(\cos(90^\circ) = 0\).

Q10: A force \(\mathbf{F} = (4\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 3\mathbf{\hat{j}}) \text{ N}\) moves a particle from origin to \(\mathbf{r} = (2\mathbf{\hat{i}}) \text{ m}\). Find the work done.

Displacement \(\mathbf{d} = 2\mathbf{\hat{i}}\) (no y-component).
\(W = F_x d_x + F_y d_y = (4)(2) + (3)(0) = 8 \text{ J}\).

Q10a: صندوق على منحدر \(30^\circ\)، كتلته \(10 \text{ kg}\). شغل الجاذبية إذا تحرك \(5 \text{ m}\) للأسفل؟

$$ W = mg\sin(30^\circ) \cdot d = 10(10)(0.5)(5) = 250 \text{ J} $$

Q10b: إذا \(\mu_k = 0.2\) في Q10a، شغل الاحتكاك؟

\(N = mg\cos(30^\circ) = 86.6 \text{ N}\)
$$ W_f = -\mu_k N \cdot d = -17.32(5) = -86.6 \text{ J} $$

Q10c: سحب صندوق بزاوية \(37^\circ\)، قوة \(50 \text{ N}\)، إزاحة \(8 \text{ m}\). الشغل؟

$$ W = 50(8)\cos(37^\circ) = 320 \text{ J} $$

Q10d: قوة لسحب \(20 \text{ kg}\) بسرعة ثابتة، \(\mu_k = 0.25\)؟

$$ F = \mu_k mg = 0.25(20)(10) = 50 \text{ N} $$

Q10e: \(\vec{F} = 5\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\)، \(\vec{d} = 2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} - \hat{k}\). الشغل؟

$$ W = 10 - 12 - 2 = -4 \text{ J} $$

Q10f: حركة دائرية بسرعة ثابتة. شغل القوة المركزية؟

صفر. القوة عمودية على الإزاحة دائماً.

Q10g: مصعد \(500 \text{ kg}\) يرتفع \(20 \text{ m}\) بسرعة ثابتة. شغل الحبل؟

$$ W = 500(10)(20) = 100 \text{ kJ} $$

Q10h: \(\theta = 120^\circ\)، \(|A| = 6\)، \(|B| = 4\). قيمة \(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}\)؟

$$ \vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 24\cos(120^\circ) = -12 $$

Q10i: رافعة ترفع \(200 \text{ kg}\) بتسارع \(2 \text{ m/s}^2\) مسافة \(15 \text{ m}\). الشغل؟

$$ W = m(g+a)d = 200(12)(15) = 36 \text{ kJ} $$

Q10j: قوة \(100 \text{ N}\)، إزاحة \(10 \text{ m}\)، شغل \(500 \text{ J}\). الزاوية؟

$$ \theta = \cos^{-1}(0.5) = 60^\circ $$

🌀 Topic 3: Springs & Variable Forces

Q11: A spring with \(k = 200 \text{ N/m}\) is stretched \(0.1 \text{ m}\). What force does the spring exert?

Magnitude \(|F_s| = kx = 200(0.1) = 20 \text{ N}\). Direction is opposite to stretch.

Q12: How much work is required to stretch the spring in Q11 from equilibrium to \(0.1 \text{ m}\)?

External work is equal to stored PE: \(W_{\text{ext}} = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = 0.5(200)(0.1)^2 = 100(0.01) = 1 \text{ J}\).

Q13: If you stretch a spring twice as far (from \(x\) to \(2x\)), how much more potential energy is stored?

Since \(U_s \propto x^2\), doubling \(x\) means \((2x)^2 = 4x^2\). The energy increases by a factor of 4.

Q14: The area under a Force vs. Position graph represents what quantity?

Work done by the force.

Q15: A spring is compressed by \(x\). Does the spring do positive or negative work on the block compressing it?

Negative. The spring pushes outwards (opposing motion) while the block moves inwards (compression). Force and displacement are opposite.

Q15a: نابضان بالتوازي \(k_1 = 100\)، \(k_2 = 200 \text{ N/m}\). الثابت المكافئ؟

$$ k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2 = 300 \text{ N/m} $$

Q15b: نابضان بالتوالي \(k_1 = 100\)، \(k_2 = 200 \text{ N/m}\). الثابت المكافئ؟

$$ k_{eq} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = 66.7 \text{ N/m} $$

Q15c: نابض \(k = 500 \text{ N/m}\) مضغوط \(0.2 \text{ m}\). الطاقة المخزنة؟

$$ U_s = \frac{1}{2}(500)(0.2)^2 = 10 \text{ J} $$

Q15d: قوة \(F(x) = 3x^2\) من \(x=0\) إلى \(x=2\). الشغل؟

$$ W = \int_0^2 3x^2 dx = 8 \text{ J} $$

Q15e: نابض من \(x_1=0.1\) إلى \(x_2=0.3\)، \(k=400\). شغل النابض؟

$$ W_s = \frac{1}{2}k(x_1^2 - x_2^2) = -16 \text{ J} $$

Q15f: جسم \(0.5 \text{ kg}\) على نابض استقر عند \(x=0.05\). قيمة \(k\)؟

$$ k = \frac{mg}{x} = 100 \text{ N/m} $$

Q15g: رسم F-x: قوة من 0 إلى 40 N على 10 m. الشغل؟

$$ W = \frac{1}{2}(10)(40) = 200 \text{ J} $$

🏃 Topic 4: Kinetic Energy & Work-KE Theorem

Q16: If the speed of a car doubles, its kinetic energy increases by what factor?

Since \(K \propto v^2\), doubling speed (\(2v\)) leads to \((2v)^2 = 4v^2\). Factor of 4.

Q17: Can kinetic energy ever be negative?

No. Mass is positive and \(v^2\) is always positive (or zero).

Q18: A net force of \(10 \text{ N}\) acts on a \(2 \text{ kg}\) object over \(5 \text{ m}\). What is the change in Kinetic Energy?

By Work-KE Theorem: \(\Delta K = W_{\text{net}} = F d = 10(5) = 50 \text{ J}\).

Q19: Two identical cars travel at the same speed. One heads North, one heads East. Do they have the same Kinetic Energy?

Yes. KE depends on speed (scalar), not velocity (vector). Direction doesn't matter.

Q20: A particle moving at \(5 \text{ m/s}\) is brought to rest by a net work \(W\). Is \(W\) positive or negative?

Negative. Final KE is lower than Initial KE, so \(\Delta K < 0\), implying \(W_{\text{net}} < 0\).

Q20a: أفعوانية تنطلق من A (h=20m) بدون سرعة. سرعتها عند B (h=0)؟

$$ v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2(10)(20)} = 20 \text{ m/s} $$

Q20b: في Q20a، سرعة العربة عند C (h=12m)؟

$$ v = \sqrt{2g(20-12)} = \sqrt{160} \approx 12.6 \text{ m/s} $$

Q20c: سيارة \(1000 \text{ kg}\) بسرعة \(20 \text{ m/s}\). شغل إيقافها؟

$$ W = -\frac{1}{2}(1000)(400) = -200 \text{ kJ} $$

Q20d: مسافة إيقاف السيارة في Q20c إذا \(F = 8000 \text{ N}\)؟

$$ d = \frac{200000}{8000} = 25 \text{ m} $$

Q20e: جسم \(5 \text{ kg}\) من السكون إلى \(10 \text{ m/s}\). قيمة \(\Delta K\)؟

$$ \Delta K = \frac{1}{2}(5)(100) = 250 \text{ J} $$

Q20f: جسمان بنفس KE. \(m_1=2\)، \(m_2=8\). نسبة \(v_1/v_2\)؟

$$ \frac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{4} = 2 $$

Q20g: قوة تضاعف سرعة جسم. كم تضاعفت KE؟

4 مرات.

Q20h: كرتان بنفس الكتلة. \(v_1=3\)، \(v_2=4\). نسبة \(K_1/K_2\)؟

$$ \frac{K_1}{K_2} = \frac{9}{16} $$

Q20i: شاحنة وسيارة بنفس KE. الشاحنة \(4\times\) الكتلة. نسبة السرعات؟

$$ v_{car} = 2 v_{truck} $$

Q20j: طائرة \(50000 \text{ kg}\) بـ \(200 \text{ m/s}\). قيمة KE؟

$$ K = 1 \times 10^9 \text{ J} = 1 \text{ GJ} $$

🌍 Topic 5: Potential Energy & Conservation

Q21: A \(1 \text{ kg}\) book is lifted \(1 \text{ m}\). What is the change in gravitational potential energy?

\(\Delta U_g = mg\Delta y = (1)(9.8)(1) = 9.8 \text{ J}\).

Q22: A ball is dropped from height \(h\). Just before hitting the ground, where has the potential energy gone?

It has converted entirely into Kinetic Energy.

Q23: Does the zero point of Potential Energy matter physically?

No. Only the difference in potential energy between two points matters for physical calculations.

Q24: Friction is a nonconservative force. What happens to mechanical energy when friction acts?

Mechanical energy is decreased and converted into Internal Energy (heat/thermal).

Q25: A pendulum swings back and forth. At the bottom of its swing, is Potential Energy max or min?

Minimum. Height is lowest, so \(U_g\) is lowest. Kinetic Energy is maximum here.

Q25a: يوضح الشكل بندول بسيط. إذا كان طول البندول \(L = 2 \text{ m}\) وأقصى زاوية \(\theta = 30^\circ\)، ما أقصى سرعة عند أسفل المسار؟

الارتفاع: \(h = L(1 - \cos\theta) = 2(1 - 0.866) = 0.268 \text{ m}\)
$$ v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2(10)(0.268)} = 2.3 \text{ m/s} $$

Q25b: كرة \(2 \text{ kg}\) تسقط من \(h = 45 \text{ m}\). سرعتها قبل الأرض؟

$$ v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{900} = 30 \text{ m/s} $$

Q25c: زحلاقة ارتفاعها \(5 \text{ m}\). سرعة طفل عند الأسفل؟

$$ v = \sqrt{2(10)(5)} = 10 \text{ m/s} $$

Q25d: كرة قذفت لأعلى بـ \(20 \text{ m/s}\). أقصى ارتفاع؟

$$ h = \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{400}{20} = 20 \text{ m} $$

Q25e: جسم \(4 \text{ kg}\) يسقط من \(10 \text{ m}\). KE قبل الأرض بـ \(5 \text{ m}\)؟

سقط 5 m: $$ K = mgh = 4(10)(5) = 200 \text{ J} $$

Q25f: نابض \(k=800\) مضغوط \(0.1 \text{ m}\) يطلق كرة \(0.2 \text{ kg}\). سرعتها؟

$$ \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 $$ $$ v = \sqrt{\frac{kx^2}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{0.2}} = \sqrt{40} = 6.3 \text{ m/s} $$

Q25g: جسم ينزلق من منحدر بدون احتكاك. هل يصل لنفس الارتفاع على الجهة الأخرى؟

نعم. حفظ الطاقة الميكانيكية يضمن عودته لنفس الارتفاع.

Q25h: في Q25g، إذا كان هناك احتكاك، ماذا يحدث؟

لا يصل لنفس الارتفاع. جزء من الطاقة يتحول لحرارة.

Q25i: مصعد يسقط من \(25 \text{ m}\). سرعته قبل الارتطام؟

$$ v = \sqrt{2(10)(25)} = \sqrt{500} \approx 22.4 \text{ m/s} $$

Q25j: سهم أطلق من قوس بسرعة \(50 \text{ m/s}\). أقصى ارتفاع إذا أطلق عمودياً؟

$$ h = \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{2500}{20} = 125 \text{ m} $$

🛡️ Topic 6: Conservative Forces & Equilibrium

Q26: If the work done by a force depends on the path taken, is the force conservative?

No. This is the definition of a Nonconservative force.

Q27: Given \(U(x)\), how do you find the force \(F(x)\)?

Take the negative derivative: \(F(x) = - \frac{dU}{dx}\).

Q28: At a point of stable equilibrium, is potential energy at a minimum or maximum?

Minimum. Like a ball at the bottom of a valley.

Q29: If a particle is at equilibrium, what is the net force on it?

Zero. Equilibrium implies \(F = 0\) (or slope of \(U\) is zero).

Q30: A force does zero work on a round trip. What kind of force is it?

Conservative Force.

Q30a: الرسم يوضح منحنى \(U(x)\). حدد نقاط الاتزان المستقر وغير المستقر.

المستقر: القيعان (valleys) - حيث \(dU/dx = 0\) و \(d^2U/dx^2 > 0\)
غير المستقر: القمم (peaks) - حيث \(dU/dx = 0\) و \(d^2U/dx^2 < 0\)

Q30b: إذا كان \(U(x) = 4x^2 - 2x^4\)، أوجد القوة \(F(x)\).

$$ F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx} = -(8x - 8x^3) = 8x^3 - 8x $$

Q30c: جسم يتحرك على مسارين مختلفين بين نفس النقطتين. الشغل بواسطة الجاذبية (A) نفسه (B) مختلف؟

(A) نفسه. الجاذبية قوة محافظة، شغلها يعتمد فقط على النقطتين.

Q30d: ما شرط الاتزان الحراري بدلالة \(U(x)\)؟

$$ \frac{dU}{dx} = 0 $$

Q30e: إذا كان \(U = 5x^2\)، ما نوع الاتزان عند \(x = 0\)؟

مستقر. لأن \(d^2U/dx^2 = 10 > 0\) (قاع).

Q30f: هل الاحتكاك قوة محافظة؟ لماذا؟

لا. شغل الاحتكاك يعتمد على المسار، ويحول الطاقة لحرارة.

Q30g: جسم ينزلق من A إلى B ثم يرجع. إذا فقد 20% من طاقته للاحتكاك، ما ارتفاعه النهائي إذا بدأ من \(h\)؟

$$ h_{final} = 0.8h $$

📊 Topic 7: Graph-Based Problems

Q31: An object moving along the x-axis is acted upon by a force \(F_x\) that varies with position as shown. How much work is done by this force as the object moves from \(x = 2 \text{ m}\) to \(x = 8 \text{ m}\)?

Options:

  • A. +50 J
  • B. Zero
  • C. -30 J
  • D. +30 J
  • E. +40 J
المفهوم: الشغل = المساحة تحت منحنى \(F_x\) مقابل \(x\). المساحات فوق المحور موجبة، والمساحات تحت المحور سالبة.
الطريقة الأولى: حساب المساحات
• المنطقة 1 (من \(x=2\) إلى \(x=6\)): مثلث فوق المحور
$$ W_1 = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2}(4)(20) = +40 \text{ J} $$ • المنطقة 2 (من \(x=6\) إلى \(x=8\)): مثلث تحت المحور
$$ W_2 = \frac{1}{2}(2)(10) = -10 \text{ J} $$
الطريقة الثانية: شبه المنحرف
يمكن حساب المساحة الكلية كشبه منحرف من x=2 إلى x=8:
المساحة الموجبة (من x=2 إلى x=6) = 40 J
المساحة السالبة (من x=6 إلى x=8) = -10 J
الشغل الكلي:
$$ W_{\text{total}} = W_1 + W_2 = 40 + (-10) = +30 \text{ J} $$
الجواب: D. +30 J ✓

Q31a: من الرسم المثلثي التالي، احسب الشغل من \(x=0\) إلى \(x=8\).

المساحة = مثلث: $$ W = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 30 = 120 \text{ J} $$

Q31b: قوة ثابتة \(F = 10 \text{ N}\) من \(x=0\) إلى \(x=5\). الشغل؟

$$ W = F \cdot d = 10 \times 5 = 50 \text{ J} $$

Q31c: منحنى F-x مستطيل: \(F=20\) من \(x=2\) إلى \(x=7\). الشغل؟

$$ W = 20 \times 5 = 100 \text{ J} $$

Q31d: رسم U-x يظهر قاعاً عند \(x=3\). ما اتجاه القوة عند \(x=2\)؟

للأمام (+x). المنحدر سالب، فـ \(F = -dU/dx > 0\)

Q31e: رسم U-x: \(U\) يتناقص من \(x=0\) إلى \(x=5\). اتجاه القوة؟

موجب (+x). لأن المنحدر سالب.

Q31f: شبه منحرف: \(F=0\) عند \(x=0\)، \(F=20\) عند \(x=4\)، \(F=20\) عند \(x=8\)، \(F=0\) عند \(x=12\). الشغل الكلي؟

$$ W = \frac{1}{2}(4)(20) + (4)(20) + \frac{1}{2}(4)(20) = 40 + 80 + 40 = 160 \text{ J} $$

Q31g: نابض يضغط بتدريج. إذا كان \(F = kx\) و \(k=100\)، الشغل لضغطه \(0.5 \text{ m}\)؟

المساحة تحت خط مستقيم: $$ W = \frac{1}{2}(0.5)(50) = 12.5 \text{ J} $$

Q31h: منحنى F-x سالب (تحت المحور): \(F=-15\) من \(x=3\) إلى \(x=6\). الشغل؟

$$ W = -15 \times 3 = -45 \text{ J} $$

Q31i: رسم K-x يظهر أن KE ثابتة. ما الشغل الكلي على الجسم؟

صفر. \(W_{net} = \Delta K = 0\)
